Understanding cardiac rehabilitation

Print / Save as PDF

What is cardiac rehabilitation?

Cardiac rehabilitation is a medically supervised program that helps people recover from certain heart conditions and procedures. It combines exercise, health education, and emotional support to help you regain strength, manage your condition, and return to daily life. It supports your treatment and does not replace medicines or follow-up care. [1,4]

Who might benefit?

Your doctor may recommend rehabilitation after a heart attack, heart surgery, or a procedure such as angioplasty. Some people living with heart failure may also benefit. Being older or having limited fitness does not automatically rule you out. Your condition, current symptoms, and medical assessment help determine whether a program is suitable and when you can safely begin. [2]

More than an exercise program

A rehabilitation plan can include supervised activity, help with food choices, and counseling about stress. Education may cover blood pressure, cholesterol, smoking, and taking medicines as prescribed. The team works with you to set goals that fit your needs. Progress may mean feeling more confident about movement or learning how to manage daily routines, at your own pace. [1,3,5]

Prepare for the conversation

Before contacting the unit, write down your main concern. Perhaps you want to know whether you can walk to the market again, return to work, or manage stairs at home. Bring these questions to your doctor. Ask whether you need a referral, which records to bring, and whether there are activities you should avoid while waiting for assessment. Keep a folder for your discharge summary, recent test results, and current medicine list. Include the names and doses of medicines and any allergies. Ask the unit which documents it requires before your visit. Confirm the appointment process, fees, session schedule, and available assistance directly. Confirm these details with the unit; arrangements vary between programs.

Let the team know what matters

Tell your care team about transport costs, work hours, caregiving duties, or difficulty understanding instructions. These concerns can affect attendance. Ask for an explanation in words you understand. If you wish, invite a trusted relative to help remember the plan. Keep a short notebook of questions and bring it to each visit so concerns do not get lost.

Know when to seek urgent care

A rehabilitation appointment is not emergency care. New chest pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or other sudden concerning symptoms need urgent medical attention. Do not wait for an email response or a routine session. Ask your doctor for a clear emergency plan and explain it to the person helping you at home. [2]

Your first step

Ask your doctor whether cardiac rehabilitation is right for you. Contact the unit to confirm appointment requirements, fees, and what to bring. Share your symptoms, medicines, and concerns so your healthcare team can help you build a suitable plan.
