Recovering at home after heart bypass surgery

Print / Save as PDF

What happens after bypass surgery?

Heart bypass surgery creates another route for blood to reach the heart muscle. Recovery takes time and differs for each person. Before you leave hospital, make sure you understand wound care, medicines, activity limits, and follow-up. Follow the plan from your surgeon and healthcare team. [1,4]

Know how to care for wounds

You may have a chest wound and wounds where blood vessels were taken from another part of the body. Ask how to clean and protect each site, whether dressings need changing, and when bathing is allowed. Follow the specific instructions you receive. Ask which changes need a call, including concerns about redness, discharge, fever, or wound opening. Do not apply a product without checking. [1,2,3]

Discuss pain and tiredness

Some pain and fatigue can occur during recovery, but the team should explain what to expect and what is concerning. Use prescribed pain relief as directed and ask before adding another product. Report pain that is new, worsening, or not controlled. Do not assume that all chest pain comes from the operation. Chest pressure or severe breathlessness may need emergency assessment. [1,2]

Clarify movement restrictions

Ask about walking, stairs, lifting, pushing, pulling, driving, and returning to work. Restrictions can depend on the operation and how you are healing. A date quoted online is not permission to resume an activity. Request clear instructions about both the early recovery period and later progression. Ask when cardiac rehabilitation should begin and who will assess your readiness. [1]

Arrange practical help

Before going home, consider meals, shopping, transport, and tasks that may exceed your temporary limits. If someone will help, explain the plan and confirm what they can realistically do. Tell the team if you live alone or must care for another person. Ask about difficulties using the toilet, getting into bed, or moving around the house. Do not wait until you are home to mention a major barrier.

Keep medicines and follow-up organized

Check which medicines continue, which have changed, and whether any have stopped. Make sure the current list matches your discharge instructions. Confirm follow-up appointments and whom to call with questions. Keep a record of concerns so the visit can address them. If cost or transport threatens attendance, contact the clinic early rather than missing the review without explanation.

Balance confidence with caution

Ask your team how to recognize progress and what you can safely do next. Report symptoms that suddenly worsen. Severe breathlessness, fainting, or suspected heart attack symptoms need emergency care. Recovery does not have to match another patient’s pace.
