Understanding your cholesterol results

Print / Save as PDF

Why does cholesterol matter?

Your body needs cholesterol, but unhealthy levels can contribute to plaque buildup inside arteries and increase the risk of a heart attack or stroke. High cholesterol usually has no noticeable symptoms. A blood test helps your doctor assess your levels alongside your overall health. [1,4]

Read results with your doctor

A lipid panel can include total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, and triglycerides. These results have different meanings. LDL is often called bad cholesterol, while HDL is often called good cholesterol. The labels are useful shortcuts, but they do not replace a full assessment. Ask what your results mean given your heart history, diabetes status, blood pressure, and other risk factors. [1,5]

Ask for your own goal

A result marked within a laboratory range may not be the right treatment goal for someone who has already had a heart event. Ask your doctor which number needs to change and why. Write down the agreed target and the planned follow-up. Avoid comparing your result with a relative’s result without considering the different health conditions and treatment plans involved.

Understand treatment choices

Your plan may combine food changes, appropriate activity, and medicine. If a cholesterol medicine is prescribed, ask what benefit is expected and what side effects to report. Tell the clinician about other medicines and supplements. Do not stop a prescribed medicine because a later result improves. An improved result may mean the treatment is working, not that you no longer need it. [2]

Choose practical food changes

Guidance for lowering cholesterol includes reducing saturated fat and building a heart-healthy eating pattern. Your nutrition counselor can help you find changes that fit your usual meals. Bring examples of what you eat and ask which adjustment is most useful. A plan based on affordable, familiar food is easier to repeat than one built around products you rarely buy. [3]

Plan follow-up tests

Ask when your next blood test is due and whether any preparation is needed. Keep a copy of the results and note medicine changes alongside them. Follow the schedule your doctor sets rather than repeating tests without advice. If you cannot afford a test or attend a visit, tell the team early so you can discuss the next step. [2]

Look beyond a single number

Cholesterol treatment is one part of heart care. At your next appointment, ask whether your current plan is reaching the intended goal and how it fits your other treatment. Keep the answer with your results.
